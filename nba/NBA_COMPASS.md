5. Standard: "extremely accurate" = calibrated at the leg level (band × direction × rung, and confidence bands that hit their stated rate) on BOTH seasons with the same recipe. A calibrated 75% still loses one time in four.
6. One fixed recipe; every value (tier cutpoints, cells, Platt, HCA, blowout lookup, minutes ratios, dispersion, zero-adjust, factor betas, phase ratios) is computed in-run from history as of the day. Nothing pasted.

**Baseline recipe (canonical file `nba/backtest/classification_ladder_v12.py`)**
13. Component model: outcome = projected minutes × per-36 rate / 36; minutes = 3-part mixture (normal / blowout / dud) on a DERIVED static spread (net rating + HCA + rest; r = 0.46 vs final margin, MAE 11.5).
14. Won blowouts are not a penalty (51.9% over-rate vs 49.1%); lost blowouts are (36.9%). B2B is a P(available) effect, not a rate effect.
15. Tiers: rate tier = quantile per-36 rate, max 24 / min 15 per tier, ranked WITHIN role tier; role tiers by competitive-game minutes (IRON_MAN >36, HUS 32–36, STARTER 27–32, ROTATION 21–27, BENCH 15–21, FRINGE <15); variation bands = per-prop line percentiles; direction is a cell dimension.
16. Probabilities: hierarchical empirical cells (tier×role×rung → band×role×rung → band×rung, min 300) over a parametric fallback. Cell mode per prop by evidence: REPLACEMENT for points, rebounds, assists, FGA, 3PA, turnovers, fouls; logit SHIFT with ordering strength λ for 3PM (1.0), blocks/steals/FTM (0.5). Shift applies the finest level only.
17. Prior strength measured, not seeded: points 25, rebounds 40, assists 20, 3PM 25, FGA 15, 3PA 20, blocks 50, steals 125, turnovers 95, fouls 100, FTM 60 (top-decile 20-game regression: STL 17%, PF 14%, TOV 13%, FTA 9%, BLK 6%, REB 4%, FGA 3%).
18. Guards: upper ceiling only (MLB's symmetric floor is a bug on a ladder); Wilson for n<30; Platt per (prop, band, role, rung) with n≥1000, A>0, max shift 0.15, pooled fallback.
19. Band mean-ratio cells only where the sign is consistent across both seasons (rebounds kept; 3PM dropped as regime).
20. Zero-adjusted NegBin for blocks/steals/turnovers/fouls (actual P(0) by mean band from train).
21. Factor layer (owner: in from the start): opponent profile (DEF rating, miss rate, OREB%, TOV%, forced TOV%, FTA rate, paint share, 3PA allowed), pace (geometric mean), home, B2B; betas fit on train in log-rate space. Verdict: real but small (Brier −0.1 to −0.3%); home and B2B ≈ 0.
22. Cross-season carryover (CARRY_N = 8) + season-phase cell (OCT/NOV/MID/APR ratios fit on train seasons with a predecessor). Without them October had zero projections.
23. 3PM is a compound model (tier on 3PA/36; makes | attempts Binomial — var ratio 0.94); FTM likewise on FTA.

85. **THE M1/B4 REJECTIONS WERE WRONG — corrected 2026-09-13.** With a properly built defender rating and INTERACTION terms, the defender factor **helps on 4 props** (points, fga, fta, pra). The earlier nulls measured a crude feature and a main-effect-only fit, not the absence of signal.
   - **What was crude**: `points allowed per possession` confounds the DEFENDER with WHO he guarded (a defender who draws the opponent's best scorer looks bad by construction — the Sloan work names such metrics unreliable, opponent 3P% having almost no year-to-year correlation); `blocks per 36` misses rim protection entirely, which is largely DETERRENCE (shots never attempted).
   - **The proper feature** — `nba_ref.defender_ratings`, 111,768 rows over both seasons at 22 weekly as-of dates: two-way ridge `Y = mu + alpha(offence) + beta(defence)` weighted by partial possessions, so beta is the defender effect NET of his opponents; five channels (`def_pts` sd 3.79/100, `def_fg` 2.58 pp, `def_3p` 1.73 pp, `def_foul` 0.68, `def_tov` 0.44); reliability `n/(n+k)` with mean 0.44 so a typical defender is shrunk more than half way to the mean; refit weekly using only prior games.
   - **ALL FOUR WINS COME FROM THE INTERACTION FORM, NOT THE MAIN EFFECT.** On PRA the main effect gains 0.005 MAE and the interaction gains **0.050 — ten times more**. The `defender × A2` term is the largest coefficient every time (+0.088 PRA, +0.213 FTA), matching the practitioner claim that books misprice when factors move TOGETHER — a key teammate injury in a favourable matchup.
   - **LESSON: a null is only as strong as the feature that produced it.** Every factor rejected on a crude feature or a main-effect-only fit must be re-tested before the rejection is believed. **Owed re-tests**: the A2 rate response (its usage multiplier ASSUMED proportional reallocation and was never fitted), B4 rim protection (needs a deterrence measure — opponent rim FGA and rim FG% with the player on the floor — not block counts), and A2's usage allocation itself.

(b) **Never change two variables and attribute the result to one** — an earlier run added player history AND split per-tier, then blamed the split without evidence; the ablation (now persisted to `factor_gate_results`) showed the split was indeed harmful but the feature was *helpful*, which the confounded test could not have established.

The chain: baseline HP → scenario selection → as-of calibration → **final_hp** → **confidence** → **score** + **edge**, written to `nba_score.final_hp`. **VERIFIED: 60/60 season-props, both seasons, ~38.7M legs, zero invalid probabilities.** Leg-level accuracy 0.5643 log-loss on 1,248,826 graded PrizePicks legs. Tier behaviour is exactly right: easy goblins **0.6996**, standard **0.5014**, hard demons **0.2167** — monotone across all seven tiers, and the standard line landing on a coin flip is the cleanest possible sanity check.
100. **THE CALIBRATION PARITY VIOLATION, FOUND AND FIXED.** `nba_score.ladder_calibration` was fitted on 2024-25 and PASTED onto 2025-26 — a constant carried between days, which `NBA_DAILY_PARITY_AND_BACKFILL.md` §5 forbids and COMPASS fact 6 rules out ("every value… computed in-run from history as of the day. Nothing pasted"). It also LEAKED on a same-season replay. Replaced by `nba_score.ladder_calibration_asof` (`nba/build_asof_calibration.py`): cells refit WEEKLY from graded legs **strictly before** each date, with the prior season's SAME-PHASE cell inherited until current evidence exists. Verified: 2024-25 has **zero** inherited cells (nothing to inherit), 2025-26 carries **2,762**; shift magnitudes stable across seasons (0.139 vs 0.144); and it does MORE work than the pasted table (0.0152 vs 0.0041 mean shift on rebounds). **That is what makes the formula valid for ANY day — a replay, a live mid-season day, and opening night all take one code path.**
101. **CONFIDENCE IS A DATA THERMOMETER, NOT A PROBABILITY** (owner directive, `nba/build_confidence_v3.py`, `nba_score.confidence_model`). It measures how far the DATA supports this leg's hit probability — factors mined and present, complete, REAL vs derived, market corroboration, player consistency, form. **Starts at 99 and loses points for NAMED deficiencies**; a fully-supported leg reads ~99 and the floor (~55) needs everything to stack against it at once. Measured mean **0.92–0.95**, which is the point: our data IS good, so confidence IS high. The 20–40% band is impossible by construction because the core factors are always present. **Three earlier versions were wrong and each failure taught something:** (a) equal-mass quartiles FORCE 25% of legs to be "low" however good the data — a ranking, not a thermometer; (b) hand-weighted pillars failed verification on 2.1M legs — existence separated NOTHING (0.0000), quality was INVERTED (−0.0028), only market backing worked (+0.0015); (c) the conformal version scored `|won−hp|/√(p(1−p))`, which is dominated by ALEATORIC noise, so a coin-flip leg with perfect data scored badly.
102. **ALEATORIC vs EPISTEMIC — the distinction that fixed confidence.** Aleatoric uncertainty is the event's own randomness and is ALREADY stated by the HP (a 0.50 probability IS "this is a coin flip"); encoding it again in confidence double-counts it. **Confidence must measure EPISTEMIC uncertainty only** — our ignorance, which better data could reduce. This also explains a measurement error worth remembering: comparing RAW |actual−stated| across confidence tiers is invalid when the tiers sit at different probabilities, because Bernoulli variance peaks at 0.50 — elite legs averaging HP 0.488 HAD to look worse than low legs at 0.692 whatever their true reliability. **Deduction weights are MEASURED, not assigned**: each factor's share comes from how much realised |gap| separates its high-value legs from its low-value legs, with a per-factor cap (redistributing the excess, not just clipping) and a floor for factors that measure no separation. Role carries the most (fringe players miss by 0.0283 vs iron-men at 0.0008 — a 35× difference). Season phase is a factor too: Oct-Nov 0.80, Dec-ASB 1.00, post-ASB 0.88, push 0.92.
103. **SCORE IS 0–100 AND CONFIDENCE *ENHANCES* IT, NEVER TAXES IT.** A straight product (`hp × conf`) KILLS good legs — 0.95 HP at 0.90 confidence scores 85.5, worse than the probability alone, which is backwards. Confidence now pivots around a **0.85 neutral**: above it the score is lifted toward 100 (up to half the remaining headroom), below it pulled down (up to 35%). Verified on live rows: 0.478 HP / 0.949 conf → **65.06** (vs 45.39 multiplicative); 0.434 HP / 0.952 conf → **62.71** OUTRANKS 0.468 HP / 0.884 conf → 52.78, so better-supported data wins at equal probability. **`edge` is now its own column** (distance above break-even) because it answers a different question — edge is "is this an opportunity", score is "how good is this leg"; the slip engine wants both.
104. **FOUR PERFORMANCE LESSONS THAT COST ~90 MINUTES EACH.** (a) **READ THE PLAN FIRST.** Three query rewrites were guessed before one `EXPLAIN` gave the answer immediately: a Parallel Hash Join was building a hash from **8,270,978 `final_hp` rows** because the join key contained `replace(...)` and `lower(regexp_replace(...))` — **a function on a join column means no index can ever be used**, which is why all four indexes built that day were irrelevant to it. (b) **Materialise, then join on plain columns** — resolve the functions into a temp table, index and ANALYZE it. (c) **Loop per prop**: one prop plans healthily, all 30 at once sorts ~19.6M rows in a single merge join. (d) **Write results INSIDE the loop** — a run that accumulates 30 props in memory and writes at the end produces NOTHING on a stall and says nothing about where it stalled. Permanent infrastructure added: `board_outcomes_nm_idx` / `rung_market_nm_idx` / `board_tiers_nm_idx` (expression indexes on the normalised-name join, 487 MB) and `baseline_history_lookup_idx` (covering, 1,334 MB) — every name join and baseline-component lookup in the system benefits. Also: `.github/workflows/nba-engine-test.yml` gives read-only tests their OWN concurrency group, because every 4-minute validation used to queue behind every 45-minute write.
105. **`final_hp` STAYS DENORMALISED — A DELIBERATE DECISION, NOT AN OVERSIGHT** (2026-09-19). The columns it shares with `baseline_history` (`game_id`, `anchor`, `band`, `phase`, `prop_tier`, `n_uncertain`, `baseline_hp`) look like ~25 GB of duplication. **They are bought speed.** Two claims made against slimming were TESTED and one of them was MY error, so both are recorded: (a) "the join duplicates rows" — **WRONG**, verified 140,130 in → 140,130 out, exactly 1:1 (one baseline row serves the Over and Under legs); I had misread a planner ESTIMATE (74,047) as an actual. (b) "32.9 seconds per day" — **misattributed**: that was COLD CACHE (`read=50707`); the same join warm shows `shared hit=28514 read=1`. **The real reason to keep it** is the owner's: slip-strategy work means DAYS of backtesting across many dates at once, which is the cold-cache case — joining would force repeated `baseline_history` reads from disk on a **2 GB RAM** server, while the denormalised table is one scan, no join. **The space cost buys backtest speed, and backtesting is the next phase.** Revisit only if the server is upgraded AND the workload stops being backtest-heavy. **Index audit (2026-09-19): every large index earns its place** — `final_hp_uidx` 259.9M scans, `baseline_history_uidx` 53.3M, `baseline_history_lookup_idx` 22.3M (built that day, validated). **TRAP: `board_outcomes_leg_uidx` shows 0 scans but is UNIQUE — it enforces no-duplicate-legs; scan count is the wrong test for a unique index.** Safe hygiene only: drop superseded tables (A2 panels and the pasted `ladder_calibration` — facts 91 and 100, done) and plain `VACUUM`. **`VACUUM FULL` on `final_hp` FAILS** — it needs free disk equal to the table size (13 GB), and the disk pressure that makes it necessary is what prevents it.

107. **TWO PIPELINES, CUTOFF 1:15 PM PT — AND THE SCENARIO SIMULATOR IS DROPPED** (owner decision 2026-09-19; supersedes the three-phase design in facts 41 and 68). **THE 2:30 PM PT ANCHOR WAS MY DRIFT, traced to its origin**: the 2026-09-09 session recorded a list of OBSERVED injury-PDF snapshot timestamps (12:30 / 1:00 / **2:30** / 3:30 / 4:00 / 6:45 / 7:45 PM) — *Eastern*, from the PDF filenames — alongside the correct policy on the same line ("game-day 11am–1pm local"). **2:30 PM ET is 11:30 AM PT.** It was then promoted to "the 2:30 PM PT day-of report" and repeated as established in facts 68, 73, 74 and 96. None of those measurements are wrong — they were computed at a *later* cutoff, so they are if anything conservative — but the ANCHOR was never a rule.
**THE REAL TIMETABLE (Pacific), every daily factor:** prior-night box scores ~3 AM · **referee assignments ~6–7 AM** · market spread/total from the 08:00 snapshot · projected lineups through the morning · boards on demand · **game-day injury report: 11am–1pm LOCAL TO EACH GAME'S MARKET, so Eastern clubs file by 10 AM PT and PACIFIC clubs are last at 1:00 PM PT.** The injury report is the binding constraint; everything else lands hours earlier. **⇒ cutoff 1:15 PM PT, one window, all legs.**
**ARCHITECTURE: (1) heavy overnight pipeline** (baseline/delta, can start ~3 AM PT) **and (2) a light 1:15 PM PT pipeline** for the day-of factors, board snapshot and rescore. **No third phase.**
**SCENARIO PRECOMPUTE IS DROPPED** — its whole value was "enumerate every availability branch now, SELECT the realised one at the later window". **With a single run there is nothing to select with, so enumeration is pure cost** (~0.5–1M rows/day). Residual availability uncertainty is carried where it belongs: N1 probability-weighted availability inside the projection, which the baseline already does. `nba_score.scenario_realised` and its calibration stay as a MEASUREMENT (fact 96 remains true about why selection beats prediction) but no longer run daily.
**DATA FRESHNESS IS DROPPED as a confidence factor** — at a single 1:15 cutoff every leg carries the same report generation, so a freshness term penalises uniformly and discriminates nothing. The uncertainty it would proxy for is already priced by N1. **Late tips do get more post-cutoff amendment than early tips, but those amendments are unusable anyway when slips are placed once at ~1:30.**
**DATA NOTE for any future re-test**: the injury backfill holds **48 hourly snapshots per game-date** (real archived PDFs, Eastern timestamps) — hourly resolution, NOT the league's 15-minute cadence, and the season crosses DST, so any cutoff analysis finer than ±1 hour needs the 15-minute archive.
**106. PRIZEPICKS PER-LEG MULTIPLIERS ARE NOT ON ANY PUBLIC SURFACE**, alongside the Sleeper ladder and Chalkboard. Ruled out exhaustively: the board payload has **zero** hits for `multiplier|payout|factor|coefficient` across 691,431 lines (a demon carries only `odds_type`, `adjusted_odds` as a BOOLEAN, and `line_score`); ~20 guessed API paths all return DataDome 403 even through the working proxy with `curl_cffi` chrome124 and a US/California egress IP; `app.prizepicks.com` is itself DataDome-walled so its bundles cannot be scanned the way the Underdog ladder was (fact 49); and **eight independent commercial scrapers all expose "odds types (standard, demon, goblin)" and NONE exposes a PP payout field — while the same vendors expose "payout multipliers" for Underdog, Sleeper and Pick6.** The factor is priced SERVER-SIDE when the entry is built, which is exactly why the app shows nothing on one leg and a multiplier on the second. **The capture to do: from a COMPUTER browser (free), log in, DevTools → Network → Fetch/XHR, add leg 1, CLEAR, add leg 2, then "Copy as cURL"** — the method that solved Underdog and Fliff. iOS cannot do it free (iOS 17+ blocks `javascript:` bookmarklets). Owner's correction to keep in view: the factor is NOT one number per tier — it varies by rung, side, prop, player form and team form, so slip-by-slip inference would need an enormous sample and never be certain.
