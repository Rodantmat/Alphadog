## Part 11 — Combined variant simulation and full bootstrap validation

- Per the principal's instruction to pursue both remaining individual threads and the combined "dampen everything unproven" variant, in the order that made sense (finish cheap individual items first, since their outcomes are inputs to the combined variant).
- Built the combined variant: baseline + only the 3 factors with real validated positive evidence (`opposing_pitcher_quality`, `schedule_travel_fatigue`, `catcher_framing`), every other factor zeroed. Tested within-cell quartile spread on the full real graded-leg population (n≈105K): baseline alone +17.68pp, current live enrichment (all ~19 factors) +5.12pp, reduced variant **+17.91pp** — matching or slightly exceeding baseline alone, recovering nearly 13pp versus the current live enrichment.
- Validated day-level robustness: comparing within-cell correlation with outcome day by day, the reduced variant beat the current full-enriched variant on 22 of 27 days, mean improvement +0.056, **t = 5.295** — by far the strongest, most significant result of the entire investigation.
- Ran the full bootstrap-equivalent validation the standing methodology requires before anything is called proven: 95% CI on the day-level improvement ≈ [0.035, 0.077] (entirely positive), and leave-one-out mean stays positive excluding any single day (range 0.0517-0.0591, zero non-positive folds). **This passes every gate in the standing methodology.**
- Updated the dossier with a new §4 documenting this in full, renumbered subsequent sections, and updated next-steps to reflect that backtest validation is complete — the next decision is how to phase toward live consideration (shadow/parallel run before touching production code), which requires the principal's review before proceeding.

## Part 12 — Critical correction: "avoids destruction" is not "proven enhancement"

- The principal directly challenged the Part 11 result: does the reduced variant actually bring *real enhancement* over baseline, tested against real outcomes — not just avoid the current enrichment's damage?
- Ran the correct test: day-level correlation between the reduced variant and real outcomes, compared directly against baseline's own day-level correlation with real outcomes (not the pooled quartile-spread comparison used in Part 11). **Result: the reduced variant does NOT beat baseline.** Beats baseline's correlation on only 9 of 27 days, mean improvement slightly negative (-0.0039), t=-1.661.
- Broke this down per factor: `schedule_travel_fatigue` shows no significant improvement over baseline (t=0.895, 17 days). `opposing_pitcher_quality` — previously documented as "the best-validated factor in the system" from a 2026-08-13 session (residual-validated at n≈67,001) — actually trends **negative** relative to baseline in this stricter test (t=-1.968, 21 days). `catcher_framing` couldn't be tested at the same per-cell sample threshold.
- **Root of the tension**: the prior "best-validated" claim for `opposing_pitcher_quality` was based on `corr(contribution, outcome − baseline)` — a valid test for "carries information baseline lacks" — which is a different, weaker claim than "adding it to baseline improves baseline's own correlation with outcome." A factor can pass the first test without clearing the second, especially with a small effect size relative to baseline's own strength. This is now documented explicitly in the dossier so it isn't re-mistaken for a contradiction or an error in either test individually.
- Corrected the dossier's §4 in full: the combined-variant finding is downgraded from "validated enhancement" to "avoids destruction, does not yet prove enhancement" — a materially different and more honest claim. What remains fully validated and unchanged: the current live ~19-factor enrichment is confirmed, repeatedly, severely destructive relative to baseline (that specific finding, and the "reduced beats current-full" comparison, both still hold — only the "reduced beats baseline" claim was wrong).

## Part 13 — CRITICAL: the baseline leakage discovery (external, independently reproduced)

- A sister chat (the original slip-calibration chat) discovered `backtest.baseline_v6_asof` at `as_of_date=D` includes day D's own game — verified on 51/51 players via `non_push_sample` matching game-log counts inclusively, not exclusively. Their re-run with a lagged (D-1) baseline showed +5.78pp within-cell spread, not +39.76pp — nearly matching this session's own earlier, independently-derived "production's own historically-recorded baseline value" of +5.21pp.
- Independently reproduced in this session: spot-checked 2 players directly (`non_push_sample` matched inclusive game counts exactly both times, not exclusive), then reran the original Part 1 quartile test with `baseline_v6_asof` joined at D-1 instead of D (light single-join query, n≈50,000): **+5.32pp** — confirming the leakage and matching both the sister chat's number and this session's own prior finding.
- **The properly-lagged baseline (+5.32pp) and the current live enrichment (+5.31pp) are now statistically indistinguishable.** This overturns the entire premise the whole investigation was built on: it was never "enrichment destroys a strong baseline" — both currently perform almost identically, and both are modest.
- Added a critical superseding-finding section to the top of the dossier. The factor-level diagnostic work (config gaps, dead code, confirmed bugs, data-quality issues) remains valid and independent of this — what doesn't survive is any pp-based claim about discrimination "destroyed" or "recovered" relative to the old +39.8pp reference point.
- Reframed mission, per the sister chat and confirmed independently: build a genuinely clean point-in-time baseline, then fairly re-measure enrichment against it, rather than assuming enrichment is guilty of destroying something that may never have existed.
